Rainfall report — May & June 2026

Northern Uganda tree-planting footprint · CHIRPS observations vs a complete 39-year record, and forecast-model scoring
Headline: May rained normally, then June collapsed. June 2026 was the driest June in 39 years of record (44 mm vs a 138 mm normal — 32%), driven by a ~2-week near-total dry spell (Jun 6–21). The next-driest June on record was 84 mm, so 2026 sits far below anything previously seen. A sudden shut-off, not a slow drought.

What it means: Every model leaned too wet — none fully anticipated the record dryness, though all were within ~2 mm/day. ICON came closest on the monthly total (47 vs 45 mm); ECMWF and GFS over-predicted by ~35–40%. Notably, NASA POWER — the satellite estimate the live dashboard uses for its "rains active" check — reads wettest (70 mm), so it over-states rainfall here vs CHIRPS. The ground truth was drier than every automated source suggested.
