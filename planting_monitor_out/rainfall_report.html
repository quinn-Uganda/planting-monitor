The 2026 dry spell — rainfall situation report

Kijani Forestry planting footprint · 20 districts · CHIRPS daily rainfall against a 46-year record (1981–2026) · as at 31 July 2026
Summary
The rains effectively stopped around 22 May and did not properly resume. Over the ten weeks to 31 July the planting footprint received 163.7 mm against a normal of 320.4 mm — just 51%.

That is the driest 22 May–31 July on record in 46 years. The previous driest was 181.8 mm in 2009. 12 of 20 districts recorded their own driest such spell on record. June alone came in at 35% of normal — the driest June in the entire record — and July, at 74%, was the #6 driest July. There was no month of normal rainfall anywhere in the window.

5. Context: the year's total is not the problem

Across 1 January to 31 July the footprint received 702 mm against a normal of 724 mm — 97%, effectively normal for the period.

This matters for interpretation: 2026 is not a low-rainfall year, it is a badly-timed one. A wet start banked a surplus that the May–July spell then erased. The shortfall landed precisely on the May–July planting window, which is why the operational impact is far larger than seasonal totals suggest. Anyone looking only at annual rainfall would conclude nothing was wrong.
Data & method. All rainfall figures are CHIRPS 2.0 (Climate Hazards Center) daily satellite rainfall — observations and historical normals alike, with no other source blended in. The record is continuous over 46 years (1981-01-01 to 2026-07-31; 16,648 daily values per district, no gaps or partial months). Figures are the 20-district footprint average unless stated; district values use that district's own grid cell and its own 46-year normal. July 2026 is CHIRPS preliminary — the final product publishes around mid-August and may shift slightly; May and June are final. Kagadi and Kyenjojo are excluded, being outside the CHIRPS extent pulled so far.
