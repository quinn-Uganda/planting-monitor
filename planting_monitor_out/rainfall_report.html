Executive summary
The season took a severe mid-year shock but is not a drought year. Rain through May ran 101 mm ahead of normal. Then June collapsed to 46 mm — 35% of normal, the driest June in 46 years, including a 14-day near-total dry spell. That one month erased the entire surplus.

Crucially, the season-to-date total is normal (596 mm vs 583 mm, 102%). This is a distribution problem, not a shortfall — the rain came, just not when seedlings needed it. Conditions have since recovered: 17 of 20 districts are GO today, and roughly 460 mm normally still falls before the window closes in mid-October. The season remains viable.

Partly. Every model leaned too wet — none fully anticipated the severity, though all stayed within ~2 mm/day. ICON came closest on the monthly total; ECMWF and GFS over-predicted by 35–40%. Note that NASA POWER, the satellite estimate behind the dashboard's "rains active" check, was wettest of all — it over-states rainfall here, so treat it as an upper bound when a planting call is marginal.

6. Where this leaves us

The season is still viable. Normally 460 mm falls between now and the mid-October cut-off (historical range 306–619 mm) — comfortably enough to establish seedlings. Conditions have recovered, with 17 of 20 districts GO today.

What June actually cost: not the year's rainfall, but a planting window. Seedlings put in the ground in early June met a 14-day dry spell at their most vulnerable stage — survival in those batches should be checked before assuming they took.

What to do now: plant into the recovery while the window is open; the useful runway is roughly ten weeks. Prioritise Acholi (Pader, Agago, Kitgum), which lost the most ground. And treat a strong start as no guarantee — this year shows a surplus can vanish in a fortnight.
