The 2026 dry spell — rainfall situation report

Kijani Forestry planting footprint · 20 districts · CHIRPS daily rainfall against a 46-year record (1981–2026) · July from ERA5 · as at 21 July 2026
Summary
The rains effectively stopped around 22 May. In the five and a half weeks that followed, the planting footprint received 57 mm against a normal of 179 mm — just 32%.

That is the driest 22 May–30 June on record in 46 years, and not narrowly: the previous driest was 107 mm in 1988, so this year came in 46% below the old record. Every one of the 20 districts recorded its own driest such spell on record.

Rain has since returned — July to date is running slightly above normal — but the spell fell squarely across the core of the May–July distribution window.

Only partly. Every model leaned too wet — none anticipated the severity. ICON came closest on the monthly total; ECMWF and GFS over-predicted by 35–40%. NASA POWER, the satellite estimate behind the dashboard's "rains active" check, read wettest of all, so treat that figure as an upper bound when a call is marginal.

5. Context: the year's total is not the problem

For completeness: across January–June the footprint received 596 mm against a normal of 583 mm — effectively normal for the half-year. A wet start banked a surplus that the May–June spell then erased.

This matters for interpretation: 2026 is not a low-rainfall year, it is a badly-timed one. The shortfall landed precisely on the distribution window, which is why the operational impact is far larger than the annual totals suggest. Anyone looking only at seasonal rainfall would conclude nothing was wrong.
Methods. Rainfall for May, June and all historical comparisons is CHIRPS 2.0 (Climate Hazards Center) daily data, stitched into a continuous 46-year record (1981–2026; 16,617 days per district, no gaps). Figures are the 20-district average unless stated; district values use that district's own grid cell and its own 46-year normal.
July uses ERA5 (via Open-Meteo) because CHIRPS currently ends 30 June; it will be restated on CHIRPS once available. Over the 45-year common period the two agree closely at the footprint scale — ERA5 is 4% drier annually (1,307 vs 1,357 mm) with near-identical wet-day counts (210 vs 211/yr) — though in July specifically ERA5 reads about 7% low, so the July figure is conservative if anything. July to date: 94 mm vs an ERA5 normal of 85 mm for the same dates (110%), ranking 28th driest of 46; ERA5 is compared only against its own history, never mixed with CHIRPS.
Kagadi and Kyenjojo are excluded — they fall outside the current CHIRPS extent. Generated 21 July 2026 from the planting-monitor pipeline.
